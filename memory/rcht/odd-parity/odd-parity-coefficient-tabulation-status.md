# Numerical coefficient lists

The displayed couplings use the metric $(+---)$ and $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$,
so $\epsilon_{0123}=-1$. They are $c_i=-c_i^R$, $d_i=-d_i^R$ relative
to the opposite orientation in Ruiz--Femenia, as recorded in the
[convention dictionary](levi-civita-convention.md). All quoted constraints
below are adapted; their homogeneous relations and parameter count are unchanged.

Ruiz--Femenia, Pich and Portoles do not print a numerical table for the eleven coefficients $c_1,ldots,c_7,d_1,ldots,d_4$. Their single-resonance, leading-$1/N_C$ short-distance matching gives five relations,

$$
4c_3+c_1=0,\qquad c_1-c_2+c_5=0,\qquad
c_5-c_6=-\frac{N_C}{64\pi^2}\frac{M_V}{\sqrt{2}F_V},
$$

$$
d_1+8d_2=\frac{N_C}{64\pi^2}\frac{M_V^2}{F_V^2}
-\frac{F^2}{4F_V^2},\qquad
d_3=\frac{N_C}{64\pi^2}\frac{M_V^2}{F_V^2}
-\frac{F^2}{8F_V^2},
$$

so the individual coefficients are not uniquely determined. In particular, $c_4$, $c_7$, and $d_4$ do not enter these VVP constraints, and $d_1$ and $d_2$ occur only through a combination. Numerical values require additional phenomenological input and a choice of the remaining flat directions [@Ruiz-Femenia:2003jdx].

Roig and Sanz-Cillero later provide a consistent set of high-energy relations and a mapping between commonly used odd-parity operator bases, but not a basis-independent numerical table of the original eleven Ruiz--Femenia coefficients [@Roig:2013baa]. Values quoted in later phenomenological papers must therefore be checked for operator basis, resonance content, normalization, and fit assumptions before being identified with these $c_i,d_i$.
