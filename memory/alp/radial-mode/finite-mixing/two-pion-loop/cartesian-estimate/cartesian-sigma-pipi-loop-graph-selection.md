# Cartesian frame: rules for the one-loop $\sigma\to\pi^+\pi^-$ graphs

Tasks 3.1 and 3.2 of the OpenSpec change `estimate-sigma-pipi-one-loop-murayama-cartesian`. This record restates, in the Cartesian fields $\varphi=f_a+s+ip$ and the invariants $\widehat S^C_{r,s}$, $\widehat P^C_{r,s}$, the rules of the polar estimate that decide which graphs enter, which loop scale each term gets, how the routing of the loop momentum is treated and how the external residues are taken. These are rules and assumptions, not results. Each rule is linked to the polar rule it corresponds to; the polar records are used for the wording of the rules only, not for vertices, graphs or traces.

**No loop integral, scalar loop function, loop coefficient or loop amplitude is computed.** Each loop enters only through the size factor $S_\Gamma(m_{\rm term}^2)^{2-r_\Gamma}/(16\pi^2)$ per term. The estimate is an order of magnitude per graph class and can miss cancellations between graphs with the same $\epsilon$ scaling; neither it nor the polar estimate is authoritative.

## Conventions

Units GeV. Conventions and notation of Alda:2026nrd and DiLuzio:2026poh, as fixed in the [Cartesian frame and tree record](cartesian-sigma-pipi-loop-frame-and-tree.md): $\epsilon=f_\pi/f_a$, the nine $U(3)$ generators normalized to trace 2 with the singlet $\sqrt{2/3}\,\mathbf 1$, all momenta incoming, a vertex is the $n$-th derivative of $\mathcal L$, $M=V$ for a contact graph. The external legs are $s$ (momentum $p_\sigma$), $\pi^+=(\lambda_{\pi^+},0)$ (momentum $p_1$) and $\pi^-=(\lambda_{\pi^-},0)$ (momentum $p_2$); the external $\sigma$ is the pole of $s$. In a one-loop graph with $r_\Gamma$ vertices on the loop, line $j$ joins vertex $j$ to vertex $j+1$ (counting from 0).

## Inputs

- Cartesian Lagrangian, quadratic data, pole vector $v_a=\big(2\epsilon\,\mathbb M^{-1}_{m_a^2}(\mathcal C)/\sqrt{N},\,1/\sqrt{N}\big)$ with $N=N(m_a^2)$, $\mathcal C=B_0\widehat P^C_{0,1}$, assumptions 1–10 and the charge rule: [Cartesian frame and tree record](cartesian-sigma-pipi-loop-frame-and-tree.md).
- Cartesian vertices up to five legs and the elimination of $\lambda$ through $m_\sigma^2$: [Cartesian vertex record](cartesian-sigma-pipi-loop-vertex-rules.md).
- Four-piece split of the meson-line residue and its large-$\epsilon$ powers: [Cartesian large-$\epsilon$ input record](cartesian-sigma-pipi-loop-large-epsilon-inputs.md).
- Polar rules, for their wording only: decisions D1–D12 of the OpenSpec change [`estimate-sigma-pipi-one-loop-murayama`](../../../../../../openspec/changes/estimate-sigma-pipi-one-loop-murayama/design.md), rules 1–5 of the [polar selection record](../one-loop-estimate/murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-graph-selection.md), the [polar meson-line piece record](../one-loop-estimate/murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-meson-line-pieces.md) and the section "Residue prescription" of the [polar external-leg record](../one-loop-estimate/murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-traces-external-legs.md).

## Rules in the Cartesian frame

| no. | Cartesian statement | polar rule |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Graph set.** The one-loop graphs are cycles of Cartesian vertices; every vertex on the cycle carries at least one external leg, because a vertex with two legs is the quadratic Lagrangian, which is entirely in the propagators (no $s$–$p$ or $s$–meson term: $\langle\widehat S^C_{1,1}\rangle=0$, $\widehat P^C_{1,0}=0$; the $p$–meson mixing is in $v_a$ and in the meson-line residue). Every graph that does not vanish identically in every routing is kept; no graph or term is dropped by a series in $\epsilon$ or $1/\epsilon$. | D10; selection rule 1 |
| R2 | **Internal lines.** Radial line: $s$ at both ends, scale $m_\sigma$. ALP-like line: $v_a$ at both ends, scale $m_a$. Meson line: residue $(\mathbf 1_9\oplus1)-v_av_a^{\rm T}$ on (mesons $\oplus$ $p$), split exactly into (i) generator sum $\mathbf 1_9$; (ii) $p$ entry $1-1/N$; (iii) mixed entries $-2\epsilon\,\mathbb M^{-1}_{m_a^2}(\mathcal C)/N$ ($p$ at one end, $\mathbb M^{-1}_{m_a^2}(\mathcal C)$ at the other, both orientations); (iv) $-4\epsilon^2\,\mathbb M^{-1}_{m_a^2}(\mathcal C)\otimes\mathbb M^{-1}_{m_a^2}(\mathcal C)/N$. | D6; meson-line piece record |
| R3 | **Loop scale per term (D3, assumption).** A term is one choice of piece on every meson line. Its scale $m_{\rm term}$ is the largest scale of its lines: $m_\sigma$ (radial), $m_a$ (ALP-like line and pieces (ii)+(iii)+(iv), which together are $(0\oplus1)-v_av_a^{\rm T}$), $m_P$ (piece (i)). $m_P$ is one symbolic mass, the largest pole mass of the nine meson-like states; a mass per generator is not possible because completeness sums over all nine generators. No other lower limit on $m_{\rm term}$. Consequence (tested): with the common scale $m_a$ and a common routing, the ALP-like line plus pieces (ii)+(iii)+(iv) of a meson line equals the bare $p$ line, so the $p$–meson mass mixing is neglected inside the loops (it is kept in the vertices and on the external legs). | D3; selection rule 5 |
| R4 | **Size rule (D1, D2, assumption).** A scalar moment with $r$ denominators is replaced by $(m_{\rm term}^2)^{2-r}/(16\pi^2)$ with the polynomial coefficient kept; $k\cdot k$ and $k\cdot p_j$ ($p_j\in\{p_\sigma,p_1,p_2\}$, the incoming momentum of an external leg, used as a basis vector; bilinearity for sums) are set to $m_{\rm term}^2$. External products are exact: $p_\sigma^2=m_\sigma^2$, $p_{1,2}^2=m_{\pi^\pm}^2$, $p_1\cdot p_2=(m_\sigma^2-2m_{\pi^\pm}^2)/2$, $p_\sigma\cdot p_{1,2}=-m_\sigma^2/2$. A 1PI graph enters with $\sum_{\rm terms}S_\Gamma(m_{\rm term}^2)^{2-r_\Gamma}T_{\rm term}/(16\pi^2)$. | D1, D2 |
| R5 | **Generator sums (D5).** The sum over the nine generators of a meson line in piece (i) is done by $U(3)$ completeness: a single trace containing the generator twice becomes twice the product of the traces of the two matrices between the two places, $2\langle A\rangle\langle B\rangle$; two traces containing the generator once each become twice the trace of the joined product, $2\langle AB\rangle$ (identities of the [frame record](cartesian-sigma-pipi-loop-frame-and-tree.md), checked there and in the graph tests). They are applied to the compact vertex expressions before any expansion. Then $\widehat P^C_{0,0}=\widehat P^C_{1,0}=0$ (matrix identities), $\langle\widehat S^C_{0,1}\rangle=\langle\widehat S^C_{1,1}\rangle=0$ (trace identities) and the charge rule are applied. | D5, D9 |
| R6 | **D4 (exact split).** In a graph whose lines are all meson lines, only the term in which every line takes piece (i) and the only power of $\epsilon$ is the single $s$ end is excluded. Criterion, checked by script in every routing: the term contains no ALP-sector letter ($\mathbb M^{-1}_{m_a^2}(\mathcal C)$, $N$, $\widehat P^C_{0,1}$, $\widehat S^C_{r,s\ge1}$, $\widehat P^C_{r,s\ge1}$), neither $\lambda$ nor $m_\varphi^2$ (checked with $\lambda$ kept in the vertices; $m_\sigma^2$ then enters only through $p_\sigma^2=m_\sigma^2$), and its ratio to $M_0$ (its $\delta Z$ for a $\pi^\pm$ leg) depends on $\epsilon$ only through $p_\sigma^2=m_\sigma^2$. The excluded terms are recorded, not estimated. Everything else is kept, including the all-generator term of graphs with more than one $s$ end. | D4; selection rule 3 |
| R7 | **D11 (assumption).** CP with $s$ even and $p$, $\Phi$ odd: $\widehat S^C_{r,s}=(-1)^s\widehat S^{C\,\rm T}_{r,s}$, $\widehat P^C_{r,s}=(-1)^{s+1}\widehat P^{C\,\rm T}_{r,s}$; flavour-diagonal sources and quark masses. They only remove the one-particle-reducible graphs in which an external leg mixes at one loop with another state ($s$ with $p$ or a neutral meson; $\pi^+$ with $K^+$); these are not enumerated. They are not imposed as matrix identities: the trace algebra keeps generic noncommuting sources. | D11; selection rule 4 |
| R8 | **Tadpoles (assumption).** The one-loop one-point functions of $\pi^0$, $\eta$, $\eta'$ and $p$ vanish under CP; the one-loop $s$ tadpole is set to zero (assumption 9 of the [frame record](cartesian-sigma-pipi-loop-frame-and-tree.md), vacuum condition $\langle{\rm Re}\,\varphi\rangle=f_a$ at one loop). Self-energies whose $\delta Z$ is exactly zero only shift the masses; the physical masses are used. | selection rule 5 (tadpoles) |
| R9 | **Routing (D12, assumption).** For each graph and each of its $2r_\Gamma$ routings $\rho$ (loop momentum on line $j$, along the line for $\rho=2j+1$ and against it for $\rho=2j+2$) the value is $\sum_tC_t^\rho t$ over the independent products of traces of words $t$ of the canonical generic-source basis, with $\lambda$ eliminated through $m_\sigma^2$ before maximizing, and $C_t^\rho=\sum_{\rm terms}S_\Gamma(m_{\rm term}^2)^{2-r_\Gamma}c^\rho_{t,\rm term}/(16\pi^2)$ (pieces summed before maximizing). The graph enters with $\sum_tC_t^{\rho_t^*}t$, $\rho_t^*$ the routing of largest $|C_t^\rho|$, sign kept. Ties of equal magnitude and opposite sign take the sign of the sum over routings; if this sum is zero, the first routing in the canonical order is used and the product is marked. Mass-dependent choices are Piecewise expressions in homogeneous inequalities between squared masses; the comparison uses $m_\sigma\ge2m_{\pi^\pm}$ (open decay). This is an upper bound per graph on the routing ambiguity of the size rule, not on the loop. The routing spread is kept as a diagnostic. | D12; selection rule 5 |
| R10 | **External residues (assumption).** $\delta Z=-\Pi'(m^2)$, with the derivative acting on the explicit $p^2$ dependence of the numerator and, for $r_\Gamma\ge2$, on one propagator (one more power of $1/m_{\rm term}^2$), term by term and in each routing: $\delta Z_{\rm term}=-\frac{S_\Gamma}{16\pi^2}\big[\theta(r_\Gamma\ge2)(m_{\rm term}^2)^{1-r_\Gamma}T_{\rm term}+(m_{\rm term}^2)^{2-r_\Gamma}\partial T_{\rm term}/\partial p^2\big]_{p^2=m_{\rm ext}^2}$, with incoming momenta $p$ and $-p$ on the two legs of the self-energy and $k\cdot p\to m_{\rm term}^2$ not contributing to $\partial/\partial p^2$. The external legs add $M_0(\delta Z_\sigma+\delta Z_{\pi^+}+\delta Z_{\pi^-})/2$ to the estimate. The sign follows the vertex convention $M=V$; the size estimate does not fix the relative signs of the loops. | residue prescription of the polar external-leg record |

## Consequences specific to the Cartesian frame

These follow from the Cartesian vertices of the [vertex record](cartesian-sigma-pipi-loop-vertex-rules.md) and are checked by the scripts:
- The only momentum-dependent vertex is the four-meson kinetic vertex. Hence a graph can depend on the routing only if one of its vertices has four legs that can all carry a meson matrix (two pseudoscalar external legs and two ALP-like or meson line ends), and the numerator of a self-energy can depend on $p^2$ only through this vertex.
- The quartic $V_\varphi$ vertices ($-6\lambda$, $-2\lambda\xi\xi$, $-6\lambda\xi^4$) need every pseudoscalar leg to enter through its $p$ component; the charged pions have none. They therefore appear only in the tadpoles of the $s$ self-energy, which do not depend on $p^2$ and give $\delta Z=0$. $\lambda$ enters the kept graphs only through the cubic $V_\varphi$ vertices $-6\lambda f_a$ and $-2\lambda f_a\xi\xi$, eliminated through $m_\sigma^2$.
- $\widehat P^C_{0,0}=0$ removes every vertex with three meson ends, and $\widehat P^C_{1,0}=0$ every $s$ vertex with three meson ends; a vertex with one charged pion and only neutral line ends without a generator vanishes by the charge rule.

## Verification

All checks are exact symbolic identities in [the graph tests](../../../../../../scripts/test_derive_sigma_pipi_cartesian_loop_graphs.py) and [the trace tests](../../../../../../scripts/test_derive_sigma_pipi_cartesian_loop_traces.py); no random or numerical matrix is used. They check: absence of a box and of a 1PI graph with a two-leg vertex; the four-piece split equals $(\mathbf 1_9\oplus1)-v_av_a^{\rm T}$ in every graph and routing; ALP-like line plus pieces (ii)+(iii)+(iv) equals the bare $p$ line in every graph with an ALP-like line and every routing; the D4 criterion (with $\lambda$ kept) and the exact removal; the quartic $V_\varphi$ statement; routing independence of the graphs without the kinetic vertex; the termwise maximum and its branches; U(3) completeness once with explicit generators and symbolic $3\times3$ matrices (never printed).
