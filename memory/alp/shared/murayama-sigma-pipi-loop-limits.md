# Limits of the size estimate of $\sigma\to\pi^+\pi^-$ at one loop

Task 4.3 of the OpenSpec change `estimate-sigma-pipi-one-loop-murayama`. This record lists what the estimate $M_1^{\rm est}$ and the ratio $R=|M_1^{\rm est}|/|M_0|$ of the [estimate record](murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-estimate.md) do not contain, and the assumptions on which they rest. It contains no value of a loop integral, scalar loop function, loop coefficient or loop amplitude. $R$ is a size ratio, not a computed correction.

## Assumptions entering the graph list

- **CP (D11, assumption).** The source terms are CP-invariant, with $\sigma$ CP-even and $a$, $\Phi$ CP-odd. Then $\langle\widehat S_{r,s}\rangle=0$ for odd $s$ (in particular $\langle\widehat S_{1,1}\rangle=0$), and $\sigma$ mixes neither with the ALP-like state nor with any neutral pseudoscalar at any loop order. The one-particle-reducible graphs in which the external $\sigma$ turns into a pseudoscalar are therefore absent. Without CP they would have to be added, with the tree propagator of the other state at $p^2=m_\sigma^2$. Statement and consequences: [selection record](murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-graph-selection.md), rule 4; [tree and legs record](murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-tree-and-legs.md), assumption 10.
- **Flavour-diagonal sources and quark masses (D11, assumption).** Under this assumption the external $\pi^\pm$ cannot mix with $K^\pm$ at one loop, so these one-particle-reducible graphs are absent. For sources with off-diagonal entries that commute with $Q$ they are allowed and are not included. The trace algebra itself is not restricted by either assumption: it is done for generic noncommuting sources.
- **Tadpoles.** Under CP the tadpoles of $\pi^0$, $\eta$, $\eta'$ and of the ALP field vanish. **Assumption:** the one-loop $\sigma$ tadpole is set to zero (a vacuum condition on $f_a$, consistent with eliminating $\lambda$ through $m_\sigma^2$).
- **Residue prescription (assumption).** The external legs enter through $\delta Z=-\Pi'(m^2)$, with the derivative acting on the explicit $p^2$ dependence of the numerator and, for $r_\Gamma\ge2$, on one propagator, each replaced by its scalar-moment size $(m_{\rm term}^2)^{2-r}/(16\pi^2)$ ([external-leg record](murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-traces-external-legs.md)). The overall sign follows the convention $M=V$; the size rule does not fix relative signs.
- **Other assumptions** of the tree and legs record: no tree-level tadpoles, no quadratic radial mixing, $\widehat\Theta_1=0$, connection-free polar frame at leading $U(3)$ chiral order, $f_\pi$ independent of $\sigma$, regular simple poles.

## Excluded chiral background (D4)

The terms in which every internal line is a meson line taking the bare $U(3)$ generator sum, and in which the only power of $\epsilon$ is the single $\sigma$ end, are not estimated (graphs 3, 15 and π2 on each charged leg). They have the scale $m_P$ (D3). They contain no letter of the ALP sector ($\mathbb M^{-1}_{m_a^2}(\mathcal C)$, $N$, $\widehat P_{0,1}$, $\widehat S_{r,s\ge1}$, $\widehat P_{r,s\ge1}$), and their ratio to $M_0$ depends on $\epsilon$ only through the kinematics $p_\sigma^2=m_\sigma^2$ (checked in the [large-$\epsilon$ graph record](murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-large-epsilon-graphs.md)). They are the chiral loops of the model without the ALP, evaluated at the decay energy. At large $\epsilon$ their ratio to $M_0$ grows at most as $\epsilon^2$ (graph 15, through $p_\sigma\cdot p_{\pi^\pm}\sim m_\sigma^2$), below $R\sim\epsilon^4$. Their size relative to the tree is set by $\max(m_P^2,m_\sigma^2)/(16\pi^2f_\pi^2)$; this is a scale, not a computed value. They matter only if they alone approach $R_{\max}$.

## Size rule and loop scale

- **Size rule (D1, D2).** Each scalar moment with $r$ denominators is replaced by $(m_{\rm term}^2)^{2-r}/(16\pi^2)$, and every product containing the loop momentum by $m_{\rm term}^2$. No dependence on the external momenta beyond the polynomial numerator is kept; the logarithms and their dependence on $m_\sigma^2$ and on the masses of the internal lines are absent.
- **On-shell cancellations.** Setting $k\cdot k\to m_{\rm term}^2$ cannot resolve cancellations that occur only on shell. The kinetic $\sigma aa$ vertex, proportional to $k_x\cdot k_y$ of its two ALP-field ends, and the $\widehat S_{1,2}$ source vertex both grow as $\epsilon^3$ and enter graphs 2, 13 and σ4 together. When the ALP-like lines are close to their mass shell and $m_\sigma\simeq m_a$, the two can cancel in the full loop, and the size rule then overstates these graphs. At large $\epsilon$, $m_\sigma^2/m_a^2\to\kappa_\sigma/A$, so $m_\sigma\simeq m_a$ holds whenever $\kappa_\sigma\simeq A$, and exactly in the up-only model ($\kappa_\sigma=A=B_0m_u$). The $\epsilon^4$ coefficients of graphs 2, 13 and σ4, and hence the up-only coefficient $-21B_0m_u/(64\pi^2f_\pi^2)$ of $M_1^{\rm est}/M_0$ ([up-only estimate record](murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-up-only-estimate.md)), are upper sizes in this sense.
- **Routing (D12, assumption).** The rule depends on the routing of the loop momentum. For each independent product of traces the routing with the largest magnitude of the coefficient is used (termwise maximum, sign kept). Ties of equal magnitude and opposite sign take the sign of the sum of the coefficients over all routings; if this sum vanishes, the first routing in the canonical order is used. This is an upper bound per graph on the routing ambiguity of the size rule, not on the true loop: $|\sum_tC_t^\rho t|\le\sum_t|C_t^{\rho_t^*}||t|$ for every routing $\rho$. It is not a bound on the full $M_1^{\rm est}$, because graphs of opposite sign can cancel. Ten graphs depend on the routing (13, 14, 15, 20, 21, 25, 26, σ4, σ5, σ6); in 80 products of traces the chosen routing depends on the masses and is stored as exact branches, and in 59 of them the tie rule with a vanishing sum fixes the branch at an isolated boundary point ([estimate record](murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-estimate.md)). The routing spread is kept as a diagnostic in the trace records.
- **Loop scale (D3, assumption).** Each term uses the largest pole scale among its lines: $m_\sigma$ (radial), $m_a$ (ALP-like), $m_P$ (bare generator part of a meson line) and $m_a$ (rest of a meson line, $(0\oplus1)-v_av_a^{\rm T}$, including the mixed entries (iii)). $m_P$ is the largest pole mass of the nine meson-like states and is kept as a symbol; giving the nine meson-like poles one scale is an approximation. Assigning $m_a$ to the rest of the meson line neglects the ALP–meson mass mixing inside the loops: with a common routing, the ALP-like line plus that part of a meson line is the bare ALP-field line. In particular the closed lines on the $\pi^\pm$ legs give $\delta Z_{\pi1}+\delta Z_{\pi2}=0$ exactly. No lower limit on the scale other than the line scales is imposed; for the all-generator term of the $\sigma$ self-energy σ6 the scale is $m_P<m_\sigma$, which gives $\delta Z\sim\epsilon^2$ at large $\epsilon$. For triangles the largest mass gives the smallest factor $(m^2)^{-1}$, so the choice is not conservative there.
- **Large $m_a$ and $m_\sigma$; validity of the chiral Lagrangian.** At large $\epsilon$ with fixed sources (as in the up-only model) $m_a^2\simeq\epsilon^2A$ and $m_\sigma^2\simeq\epsilon^2\kappa_\sigma$ grow, and the size factors of the graphs with an ALP-like or radial line grow with them. The leading-order chiral Lagrangian is then used at $m_a$ and $m_\sigma$ of order 1–2 GeV, beyond its usual range of validity ($m_\sigma\lesssim4\pi f_\pi$). This is a limit of the estimate.
- **Validity of $R\ll1$.** $R\sim\epsilon^2m_\sigma^2/(16\pi^2f_\pi^2)=m_\sigma^2/(16\pi^2f_a^2)$ up to source factors, i.e. $R\sim\epsilon^4\kappa_\sigma/(16\pi^2f_\pi^2)$. So $R\ll1$ requires $m_\sigma\ll4\pi f_a$, which bounds $\epsilon$ from above, and the large-$\epsilon$ results apply only below that bound. Since $f_a<f_\pi$ for $\epsilon>1$, the condition $R\ll1$ fails before the condition $m_\sigma\lesssim4\pi f_\pi$ of leading-order chiral perturbation theory. This is a limit of the estimate.
- **Near degeneracy.** $\mathbb M^{-1}_{m_a^2}(\mathcal C)$ has poles at the unmixed meson masses. Near an ALP–meson degeneracy the pole vector and $N(m_a^2)$ change rapidly. The formulas stay exact there, but a single loop scale per line is not adequate ([finite-mixing limits record](alp-lo-finite-mixing-limits.md), [crossing validity record](alp-loop-crossing-validity.md)).

## Absorptive parts and thresholds

At $p_\sigma^2=m_\sigma^2$ the $\pi\pi$ channel, and depending on the masses also $K\bar K$, $\eta\eta$, $a\pi$ and $aa$, are open. The size rule gives a magnitude only and carries no absorptive part. It can miss an enhancement near thresholds or from rescattering of the final pions, in particular the $S$-wave $\pi\pi$ rescattering at $s=m_\sigma^2$.

## Cancellations between graphs

$M_1^{\rm est}$ is a signed sum of graphs with the same size factor. The relative signs follow from the vertex rules, but the size rule does not fix the relative magnitudes of different loop functions, so graphs with the same size factor can cancel or add in the full calculation. The conditions are those of the [cancellation record](alp-loop-estimate-cancellations.md). The large-$\epsilon$ powers of the [large-$\epsilon$ graph record](murayama-sigma-pipi-loop-large-epsilon-graphs.md) are given for orientation only; the estimate is the full exact sum and is valid for any $\epsilon$.

## Naturalness and counterterms

The terms not computed, including the counterterms needed to absorb the divergences of the loops, are assumed to be of the natural size given by the size rule, as in the [naturalness record](alp-loop-naturalness-assumption.md) and the [radial naturalness record](murayama-radial-naturalness-assumption.md). No counterterm basis is written and no absorption or renormalization-scale analysis is done. A counterterm of unnaturally large or small size would change $R$.

## Verification

The record states limits and assumptions only. It contains no value of a loop. The statements on D4, the routing maximum, the large-$\epsilon$ behaviour and the up-only coefficient are checked by the scripts cited in the linked records.
