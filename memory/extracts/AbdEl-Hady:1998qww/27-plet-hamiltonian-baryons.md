# 27-plet weak Hamiltonian and baryon realization

AbdEl-Hady:1998qww, Sec. 2, Eq. (26), writes the $|\Delta S|=1$, $|\Delta I|=3/2$ weak Hamiltonian as

$$
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(27_L,1_R)}=
\frac{G_F}{\sqrt 2}V_{ud}^*V_{us}\frac{c_1+c_2}{3}
\mathcal O_{|\Delta I|=3/2}^{(27_L,1_R)}+\mathrm{h.c.},
$$

$$
\mathcal O_{|\Delta I|=3/2}^{(27_L,1_R)}=
4T_{jk,lm}(\bar\psi_L^j\gamma_\mu\psi_L^l)
(\bar\psi_L^k\gamma^\mu\psi_L^m).
$$

The tensor is symmetric under $j\leftrightarrow k$ and $l\leftrightarrow m$ in the stated components, with nonzero entries

$$
T_{12,13}=T_{21,13}=T_{12,31}=T_{21,31}=\frac12,
\qquad T_{22,23}=T_{22,32}=-\frac12.
$$

The source defines $\xi=e^{i\phi/(2f)}$ with transformation $\xi\to L\xi U^\dagger=U\xi R^\dagger$, where $U$ is the vector-subgroup compensator. The octet matrix obeys $B_v\to U B_v U^\dagger$. Thus $\xi B_v\xi^\dagger$ transforms as a left-handed flavor matrix. Eq. (27) gives the leading octet-baryon realization

$$
\mathcal L^w_{27,BB}=\beta_{27}T_{ij,kl}
(\xi\bar B_v\xi^\dagger)_{ki}
(\xi B_v\xi^\dagger)_{lj}+\mathrm{h.c.},
$$

and one decuplet bilinear operator with coefficient $\delta_{27}$. The article states that this is the unique leading-order realization in the octet-baryon sector and that no mixed octet-decuplet operator occurs at this order. It absorbs $G_F$, CKM factors, and Wilson coefficients into $\beta_{27}$ and $\delta_{27}$, unlike the displayed partonic Hamiltonian.

To translate its octet dressing into the convention of Alda:2026nrd, use $\bm u\to\bm h\bm u\bm g_L^\dagger$ and $\bm{\mathcal B}\to\bm h\bm{\mathcal B}\bm h^\dagger$. Then $\bm u^\dagger\bm{\mathcal B}\bm u$ transforms as a left-handed matrix, $\bm g_L(\bm u^\dagger\bm{\mathcal B}\bm u)\bm g_L^\dagger$. The 27-plet coefficient tensor must be treated as a left-handed spurion and transformed in its 27 representation; the fixed physical tensor selects the desired $\Delta S$ and $\Delta I$ component. The replacement $\xi B_v\xi^\dagger\mapsto\bm u^\dagger\bm{\mathcal B}\bm u$ translates only the dressing convention; tensor index placement and complex conjugation must be kept consistent when mapping components.
