# Spin-One Representations

Massive spin-one resonances can be represented by antisymmetric tensors or vector (Proca-type) fields. Their field-redefinition equivalence requires the accompanying local terms to be retained; a finite, unmatched operator truncation need not look equivalent representation by representation. QCD short-distance conditions are a separate input used to make the induced $O(p^4)$ resonance couplings model independent in the comparison of representations. [@Ecker:1989yg]

The antisymmetric-tensor representation makes the leading couplings

$$
\frac{F_V}{2\sqrt2}\left\langle V_{\mu\nu}f_+^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle,
\qquad
\frac{iG_V}{\sqrt2}\left\langle V_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\right\rangle
$$

particularly economical and yields the appropriate low-energy $O(p^4)$ contributions at tree level. This convenience is a representation choice, not a distinct physical hypothesis about the vector particles. [@Ecker:1989yg]
