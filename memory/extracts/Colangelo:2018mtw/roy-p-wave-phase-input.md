# Phase solution and uncertainty

The two-pion dispersive analysis uses the elastic isovector $P$-wave phase
$\delta_1^1(s)$ from a Roy-equation solution. At matching energy
$\sqrt{s_m}=1.15\,\mathrm{GeV}$, the phase solution has two free parameters,
which can be taken as $\delta_1^1(s_0)$ and $\delta_1^1(s_1)$ at
$\sqrt{s_0}=0.8\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $\sqrt{s_1}=1.15\,\mathrm{GeV}$.
The reference estimates are $108.9(2.0)^\circ$ and $166.5(2.0)^\circ$.
The full Roy solution depends on 27 inputs; the phase-specific uncertainty
is assessed by varying the remaining Roy parameters, while the two matching
values can be fitted to $e^+e^-\to\pi^+\pi^-$ data
[@Colangelo:2018mtw, Sec. 3, Eq. (3.1), Fig. 3].

After fitting the pion vector form factor data, the phase values are
$\delta_1^1(s_0)=110.4(1)(7)^\circ$ and
$\delta_1^1(s_1)=165.7(0.1)(2.4)^\circ$; their fit and systematic
correlations are 0.66 and 0.83, respectively
[@Colangelo:2018mtw, Sec. 6, Eqs. (6.1)--(6.2)]. These postfit values are
distinct from the prefit Roy-input estimates $108.9(2.0)^\circ$ and
$166.5(2.0)^\circ$ in Eq. (3.1).

Above about $1.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, the phase continuation is less constrained.
The analysis tests alternative continuations to the asymptotic value $\pi$
and varies the continuation point, treating the result as a separate
systematic source [@Colangelo:2018mtw, Sec. 3, Eqs. (3.2)--(3.4), Fig. 4].

# Use in the three-pion baseline

The $3\pi$ dispersive analysis adopts this Roy/VFF phase for its KT solution
and a $2.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ cutoff. It reports phase and cutoff variations to
be small relative to its other systematic uncertainties
[@Hoferichter:2019mqg, Sec. 2 after Eq. (12)]. This assessment applies to
that published HVP analysis; it does not establish negligible impact on the
two VPPP coefficient combinations in a different fit.

# Relation to the prepared Madrid alternatives

The prepared alternatives in
[the phase-input record](../../../rcht/odd-parity/vppp-pipi-phase-input.md)
use the global Madrid/GKPY parameterization, which approximates the
constrained-fit-to-data input. That paper states its phase is compatible with
the more recent Roy/VFF determination within uncertainties, with a maximum
difference below $1.5^\circ$ near $0.8\,\mathrm{GeV}$
[@Pelaez:2019eqa, Sec. III.B and footnote 1]. The two phase prescriptions
are therefore related but not identical. Reproducing the published $3\pi$
fit requires the Roy/VFF input; a new fit may use the Madrid alternative if
the phase and continuation sensitivity of the fitted combinations is
quantified.
