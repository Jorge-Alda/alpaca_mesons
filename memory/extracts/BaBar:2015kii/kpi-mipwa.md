BaBar extracts the $I=1/2$ $K\pi$ $S$ wave in $\eta_c\to K\bar K\pi$ with a model-independent partial-wave analysis. The mass range is divided into 30 bins of width $60\,\mathrm{MeV}$, with an independently fitted magnitude $a_j$ and phase $\phi_j$ in each bin. The reference convention is $a_{14}=1$ and $\phi_{14}=\pi/2$ at $m_{K\pi}=1.45\,\mathrm{GeV}$. [BaBar:2015kii, Sec. V and Table VI]

For the spin-zero $\eta_c$ parent, angular-momentum conservation requires the intermediate resonance to have helicity zero, and the isobar angular factor is $Y_J^0(\cos\theta)$. The paper also states that the bachelor kaon is in orbital $S$ wave relative to the relevant $K\pi$ system and therefore does not affect the measured amplitudes. [BaBar:2015kii, Secs. IV--V, Eqs. (2)--(3)]

The measured production amplitude is not normalized as a $K\pi$ scattering partial wave. In comparing it with LASS, BaBar converts the scattering amplitude to an invariant amplitude by multiplying it by $m(K\pi)/q$ and then applies an arbitrary normalization and phase. [BaBar:2015kii, Sec. VIII]

The paper compares the measured $K\pi$ $S$-wave magnitude and phase with LASS and marks the $K\eta$ and $K\eta'$ thresholds in Fig. 13. [BaBar:2015kii, Sec. VIII and Fig. 13]

For the comparison, BaBar multiplies the LASS scattering amplitude by $m(K\pi)/q$ to convert it to an invariant amplitude, normalizes its magnitude at $1.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, and shifts its absolute phase by $-0.6$ radians. The paper reports similar phase behavior among BaBar, LASS, and E791 up to about $1.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, but striking differences in their mass-dependent magnitudes. [BaBar:2015kii, Sec. VIII and Fig. 14]
